PHASE 2 OBJECTIVE
Implement neutral, structural explanatory narratives that answer:

“Why does this exposure exist, given the decision we already approved?”

Phase 2 explains exposure backwards (from decision facts), never forwards (toward action).
Phase 2 exists to support institutional understanding, not governance enforcement.

HARD CONSTRAINTS (NON‑NEGOTIABLE)
PH2‑HC1: DERIVED‑ONLY
- Narratives MUST be derived exclusively from:
  - ADS (context, capability scope, risk acknowledgement)
  - ECP (constraint categories)
  - Phase 1 exposure structures
- No runtime data, delivery data, ownership data, or system inventory may be introduced.

PH2‑HC2: READ‑ONLY
- Phase 2 MUST NOT create, edit, advance, or delete any artefact.
- Phase 2 introduces no mutation paths of any kind.

PH2‑HC3: SINGLE‑DECISION SCOPE
- Phase 2 narratives are rendered for ONE decision at a time.
- No aggregation or comparison across decisions is permitted.

PH2‑HC4: NO PRESCRIPTION
- Narratives MUST NOT contain:
  should, must, fix, mitigate, improve, reduce, optimise, optimize,
  high risk, severe, critical, recommended.
- The governance language guard applies in full.

PH2‑HC5: STRUCTURAL EXPLANATION ONLY
- Narratives explain:
  context → cause → exposure
- Narratives MUST NOT express:
  judgement, urgency, desirability, or expectation.

PH2‑HC6: NO PERSISTENCE
- Narratives are NOT stored.
- They are regenerated deterministically from frozen artefacts on every render.


STANDARD NARRATIVE GRAMMAR (MANDATORY)
All Phase 2 narratives MUST follow this exact structure:
Context Clause
→ Structural Cause Clause
→ Exposure Surface Clause
→ Framing Boundary Clause

1. Context Clause
Anchors explanation in decision facts.
Pattern:

“Because this decision involves …”

Allowed inputs:

organisation type
sensitivity level
system intent
expected lifespan
capability scope


2. Structural Cause Clause
Explains why exposure is inevitable, not optional.
Pattern:

“…and because it requires …”

Allowed inputs:

ECP constraint categories
component categories (abstracted, not technical)


3. Exposure Surface Clause
Names where pressure appears, not outcomes.
Pattern:

“…this places sustained pressure on …”

Allowed inputs:

governance domains
impact surfaces
organisational functions


4. Framing Boundary Clause (MANDATORY)
Closes interpretation and prevents action bias.
Pattern:

“This reflects structural exposure inherent to the approved decision, not a judgement or requirement.”

This clause MUST be present in every narrative.
