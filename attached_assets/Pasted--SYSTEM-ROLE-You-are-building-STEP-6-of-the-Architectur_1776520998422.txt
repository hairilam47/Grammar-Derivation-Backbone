
SYSTEM ROLE
You are building STEP 6 of the Architecture Decision Canvas.
STEP 1 (Architecture Grammar Engine), STEP 2 (Context & Capability Declaration), STEP 3 (Trade‑Off Exploration), STEP 4 (Freeze Decision & Export — ADS / ECP), and STEP 5 (Portfolio Governance View) are COMPLETE, APPROVED, and FROZEN.
STEP 6 introduces a Policy Signals & Institutional Memory Layer.
STEP 6 exists to record, preserve, and contextualise governance‑relevant patterns observed across approved architecture decisions over time.
STEP 6 MUST NOT:

make or influence decisions
recommend actions or outcomes
claim or assess compliance
enforce rules or block changes
optimise portfolios or behaviours
integrate with delivery or execution systems
All outputs are declarative, descriptive, and governance‑oriented only.


STEP 6 OBJECTIVE
Implement a quiet, persistent institutional memory that allows leadership bodies (Board, Risk Committee, Architecture Board, Shariah Committee, or equivalent) to:

recognise recurring patterns across decisions
formally acknowledge governance‑level concerns
preserve how those concerns were discussed and accepted
STEP 6 answers the question:

“Which decision patterns were important enough for leadership to notice and remember?”


STEP 6 does not answer:

what actions to take
which systems or teams are responsible
whether the institution is compliant or non‑compliant
STEP 6 is descriptive, not prescriptive.


SECTION 3 — HARD CONSTRAINTS (NON‑NEGOTIABLE)
HARD CONSTRAINTS — MUST BE ENFORCED

1. DERIVED‑ONLY LAYER
   - STEP 6 MUST only consume observations derived from STEP 5.
   - STEP 6 MUST NOT read raw decision inputs, delivery data, or external systems.

2. NO DECISION AUTHORITY
   - STEP 6 MUST NOT influence decisions at any step.
   - Decisions MUST proceed regardless of STEP 6 signal state.

3. NO ENFORCEMENT OR BLOCKING
   - STEP 6 MUST NOT gate, block, escalate, or require acknowledgement to proceed.

4. NO COMPLIANCE CLAIMS
   - STEP 6 MUST NOT assert compliance, non‑compliance, or risk mitigation.
   - Regulatory references are contextual only.

5. NO OPTIMISATION OR RANKING
   - STEP 6 MUST NOT rank signals, prioritise concerns, or compute scores.

6. NO DELIVERY INTEGRATION
   - STEP 6 MUST NOT integrate with Jira, tickets, timelines, or execution tools.

7. GOVERNANCE LANGUAGE ONLY
   - Language MUST be neutral, descriptive, and suitable for audit contexts.




SECTION 4 — POLICY SIGNAL TAXONOMY (FIXED)
STEP 6 MUST support exactly seven Policy Signal categories. No others are permitted.
A. Risk Accumulation
B. Complexity Accumulation
C. Dependency Concentration
D. Posture Drift
E. Control Load
F. Decision Volatility
G. Exception Normalisation



Categories MUST remain domain‑agnostic.
Categories MUST NOT be extended for specific industries.


SECTION 5 — POLICY SIGNAL OBJECT (CANONICAL)
PolicySignal
------------
signalId
signalCategory        // One of the seven fixed categories
signalTitle           // Short, neutral label
signalDescription     // Plain governance language

evidenceSummary
  - source: Step 5 observations
  - observationWindow
  - numberOfADS
  - qualitativePatternDescription

interpretationGuidance
  - questions leadership should ask only

regulatoryContext     // Optional, free‑text, non‑binding
  - e.g. "Technology risk governance expectations"

status                // Observed | Under Discussion | Acknowledged
reviewingBody         // Board, Risk Committee, Architecture Board, etc.
createdAt
lastReviewedAt


Policy Signals MUST NOT include:

scores
thresholds
targets
actions
recommendations


SECTION 6 — POLICY SIGNAL LIFECYCLE
STEP 6 MUST support only the following lifecycle:
Observed → Under Discussion → Acknowledged


Lifecycle rules:

No additional states are allowed.
Signals MUST NOT auto‑advance.
Signals MUST NOT auto‑close or expire.
Lifecycle transitions MUST be human‑initiated.


SECTION 7 — SIGNAL CREATION RULES

Policy Signals are created only when leadership chooses to record concern.
Signals MUST NOT be auto‑generated by heuristics or AI.
Step 6 MAY assist discovery (e.g. reference to Step 5 views), but declaration remains human.


SECTION 8 — PRESENTATION & SEMANTICS

STEP 6 presentation MUST feel quiet and non‑urgent.
Language MUST avoid: "should", "recommended", "optimal", "priority", "fix", "resolve".
Interpretation guidance MUST be phrased as questions only.
Example:

“What does the accumulation of this pattern suggest about our current governance posture?”




SECTION 9 — FORBIDDEN FEATURES (EXPLICIT)
STEP 6 MUST NEVER include:

recommendations or next‑step suggestions
automated alerts or notifications
compliance status or scoring
delivery tracking or workflow integration
signal ranking or severity grading
AI‑generated judgement
Any feature violating these rules is OUT OF SCOPE by definition.


SECTION 10 — STOP CONDITION (DEFINITION OF DONE)
STOP IMPLEMENTATION WHEN:

At least one Policy Signal can be created, reviewed, and acknowledged
Signals persist and remain legible over time
No signal influences decisions or delivery
All acceptance criteria for Step 6 are satisfied
No further automation or optimisation is permitted without explicit institutional re‑approval.
